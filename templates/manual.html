1. Before you begin

Three ideas explain almost everything about how this system behaves. Understanding them now will save you a great deal of confusion later.

Everything posts to one ledger

Every purchase, sale, return, payment, receipt, expense and depreciation charge is written into a double-entry general ledger at the moment you save it. Every report — Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Trial Balance, Cash Flow — is added up from that ledger and nowhere else.

Why this matters to you If something has not been posted, it does not exist as far as the reports are concerned. This is the single most common source of "the numbers look wrong" — and the answer is almost always that a document was never saved, or was reversed.
Posted documents are reversed, never deleted

Once an invoice is saved it cannot be edited or deleted. If it is wrong, you reverse it: the original stays where it is, marked reversed, and the system posts a cancelling entry next to it. Both remain in the books for ever.

Why This is what an audit trail is. A system that lets you quietly delete an invoice you have already given to a customer is not keeping books — it is keeping a rough draft.
Stock is valued at weighted average

Buy 10 units at 100 and later 10 at 140, and you now hold 20 units worth 2,400 — an average of 120 each. Sell one and its cost is 120: not 100, not 140. Once goods enter the warehouse they lose their individual identity.

This is not FIFO and not LIFO. It is the same figure the Inventory account in the ledger is posted, which is exactly why the stock report and the balance sheet always agree.

2. First-time setup

Do these in order. Each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Check the chart of accounts. Accounting → Chart of Accounts
    It is created for you on first run. You may add your own expense accounts (Rent, Salaries, Utilities and so on). You cannot delete the accounts the system posts to.
  2. Check the fiscal year. Accounting → Periods
    The current calendar year and its twelve months are created for you. Nothing can be posted on a date that falls outside an open period.
  3. Set up your cash and bank accounts. Accounting → Cash & Bank Accounts
    Four are provided (Cash, Bank, Cheque, Online). Rename them or add your own — one per real bank account you hold.
  4. Enter your categories and items. Inventory → Categories, then Items
    Set a reorder level on each item so the system can warn you before you run out.
  5. Enter your suppliers and customers. Parties → Suppliers / Customers
  6. Enter opening balances — see the next section. Do this before you record a single transaction.

3. Opening balances

An opening balance is what you already owned or owed on the day you started using this system. There are four kinds, and each is entered in a different place.

WhatWhereMeaning
Cash / bankCash & Bank Accounts → editMoney already in the till or the bank.
SupplierParties → Suppliers → editWhat you already owed them.
CustomerParties → Customers → editWhat they already owed you.
StockInventory → Items → editGoods already on the shelf, and what they cost you.
These are real accounting entries, not just numbers on a form Each one is posted to the ledger against Opening Balance Equity. That is why they show up on the Balance Sheet, and why correcting one later does not double it — the system reverses the old entry and posts a fresh one.
Enter these first Enter opening balances before you record any trading. They can be corrected afterwards, but it is far easier to start from a true picture than to reconcile back to one.

When you are done, open the Balance Sheet. It should show exactly what you own and owe, and it must say Balanced.

4. Buying

Purchase invoice

Transactions → Purchase → New

Choose the supplier and the date, then add one line per item: quantity, price, and optionally a discount and a tax rate. Save.

What happens the moment you save:

  • Stock goes up, and the item's value goes up by what you actually paid.
  • The supplier's ledger shows a new payable.
  • A journal entry is posted: Dr Inventory, Cr Accounts Payable.
  • The invoice gets its number — PUR-2026-000001 — and that number is now permanent.
Purchase orders and quotations

Transactions → Purchase Orders / Quotations — these are not accounting documents. They move no stock and post nothing. They are intentions, and they become real only when converted into a purchase or a sale.

5. Selling

Transactions → Sale → New

Same shape as a purchase. On save:

  • Stock goes down. The cost of what left is the weighted average, and that becomes Cost of Goods Sold.
  • The customer's ledger shows a new receivable.
  • Two journal entries are posted: Dr Receivable, Cr Sales — and Dr Cost of Goods Sold, Cr Inventory.
  • The invoice is numbered INV-2026-000001.
Your profit is calculated at the moment of sale Not at month end, not by a report. The sale itself records what the goods cost, so the Profit & Loss is right the second the invoice is saved.

You cannot sell stock you do not have. The system will refuse and tell you how many are on hand.

6. Money in and out

Paying a supplier

Transactions → Supplier Payments — choose the supplier, the amount, and which cash or bank account the money came out of. You may link the payment to a specific bill, or leave it against the account generally.

Receiving from a customer

Transactions → Customer Receipts — the mirror image.

Expenses

Transactions → Expenses — rent, salaries, electricity. Choose an expense category; that is what decides which account it lands in on the Profit & Loss.

Always pick the right cash or bank account It is what makes the Cash & Bank page, the Cash Flow Statement and the Balance Sheet agree with your actual bank statement. Getting it wrong is not a display problem — the money is recorded as leaving the wrong place.

7. Returns

Transactions → Purchase Return — goods you send back to a supplier.
Transactions → Sale Return — goods a customer sends back to you.

A return is always made against the original invoice line, and you can never return more than was bought or sold on it.

A sale return comes back in at the cost it left at Not at today's average. If it went out costing 120 it comes back in at 120, even if the average has since moved. Anything else would invent or destroy profit that never existed.

Use a return when goods physically moved. Use a reversal (section 14) when the document itself was a mistake.

8. Inventory

Stock Valuation

Reports → Stock Valuation — what is in the warehouse right now and what it cost. It takes no date range, because stock on hand is a snapshot, not a period.

This total is the Inventory line on the Balance Sheet The two are drawn from different places and must agree. If they ever do not, something is genuinely wrong.
Stock adjustments

Inventory → Stock Adjustment — for stock that changed without a purchase or a sale.

TypeDirectionWhen
Stock InInGoods found or received outside a purchase.
Count Correction (Increase)InA stocktake found more than the system said.
Count Correction (Decrease)OutA stocktake found less than the system said.
Damage Write-offOutGoods broken or spoiled.
Sample / Free IssueOutGiven away.
Stock OutOutAnything else that left.

Every adjustment is valued at the weighted average and posted to the ledger, so it shows in your profit. Choose the type honestly — a shortfall found at stocktake is a count correction, not damage.

9. Fixed assets

A fixed asset is something you bought to use, not to sell — a van, a computer, furniture. It is not an expense; it is an asset that wears out slowly, and depreciation is how that wear reaches your profit.

Adding an asset

Accounting → Fixed Assets → New Asset

  • Cost — what you paid.
  • Salvage value — what it will be worth when finished with. Often 0.
  • Method — Straight Line (the same amount every month) or Reducing Balance (a percentage of what is left).
  • Useful life — in months, for straight line.
  • Paid from — the cash or bank account the money left.

Posts Dr Fixed Assets, Cr Cash. Cash goes down; it is not an expense.

Running depreciation

Accounting → Fixed Assets → Run monthly depreciation — choose the month and post. Every eligible asset is charged in one entry: Dr Depreciation, Cr Accumulated Depreciation.

A month can only be posted once. Run it again and the system will tell you there is nothing to depreciate. Do it once a month, at month end.

Disposal

When you sell or scrap an asset, record the disposal and what you got for it. The system works out the gain or loss against its book value and posts it.

10. The general ledger

Manual journal entries

Accounting → Journal Entries → New Entry — for things no document covers: an owner putting money in, a bank charge, an accrual.

Debits must equal credits. The system will not accept an entry that does not balance.

Some accounts are missing from the dropdown, deliberately Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and Inventory cannot be chosen. They are control accounts: they belong to the customer, supplier and stock ledgers, and they move only when a document moves them. If you could post to them by hand, the ledger and the customer's statement would stop agreeing and there would be no way to tell which was right.
Reversing a journal entry

Open it and press Reverse. A mirror entry is posted; both stay in the books. A reversal cannot itself be reversed, and an entry cannot be reversed twice.

12. Month end

  1. Enter every invoice, payment and expense for the month.
  2. Run depreciation for the month — Accounting → Fixed Assets.
  3. Open the Reconciliation report. It must be green. If it is not, stop and find out why before doing anything else.
  4. Check the Balance Sheet says Balanced, and read the Profit & Loss.
  5. Download a backup.
  6. Optionally close the period (Accounting → Periods) so the month's figures can no longer change.

13. Year end

Do everything in the month-end list, then Accounting → Periods → Close Year.

Closing a year does two things:

  • Income and expense accounts are emptied into Retained Earnings, so the new year's Profit & Loss starts from zero.
  • The year is locked. Nothing more can be posted into it.
The Balance Sheet carries forward untouched That is the point of closing. Your profit stops being "this year's profit" and becomes part of the accumulated worth of the business.

Invoice numbering restarts at 1 for the new year: INV-2027-000001.

14. Fixing mistakes

You cannot edit or delete a posted document. You reverse it — open it and press Reverse — and then enter it again correctly.

Reversing undoes everything the document did:

  • a cancelling journal entry is posted;
  • stock goes back to where it was;
  • the customer's or supplier's ledger is corrected;
  • the document is marked Reversed and its invoice prints with a REVERSED watermark.
Some reversals are refused, and rightly You cannot reverse a purchase whose goods have already been sold — they are with the customer and cannot be handed back to the supplier. Reverse the sale first, or raise a purchase return instead. Likewise, an asset's purchase cannot be reversed once it has been depreciated; dispose of it instead.

Return or reverse? If the goods physically moved, it is a return. If the document should never have existed, it is a reversal.

16. Backup & restore

Admin → System

Download Backup writes every table in the database to a single file. Keep it somewhere that is not the same computer.

Restore replaces everything currently in the system with the contents of a backup file.

Take a backup every week, and before anything irreversible It is the only way back from a mistake. If the database is ever lost, an old backup is the difference between a bad afternoon and a destroyed business.

18. Rules & limits

What the system will not let you do
  • Edit or delete a posted document.
  • Sell, issue or write off stock you do not have.
  • Reverse a purchase whose goods have already been sold.
  • Post a journal entry that does not balance.
  • Post by hand to Receivables, Payables or Inventory.
  • Post into a closed period or a closed year.
  • Delete a numbered invoice — that would leave a gap in the sequence.
  • Depreciate the same month twice.
What it does not do
  • FIFO or LIFO costing — stock is weighted average.
  • Multiple currencies.
  • Multiple companies — one set of books per installation.
  • Budgets, cost centres, or recurring entries.

19. Troubleshooting

"The Profit & Loss shows nothing"

Check the date range. It defaults to 1 January until today, so anything dated in the future — or in a previous year — falls outside it.

"An expense I entered is not in the Profit & Loss"

Check it was not reversed, and check its date is inside the range you are looking at.

"The Balance Sheet does not balance"

This should be impossible. If it happens, run the Reconciliation report, take a backup, and report it — it means something is genuinely wrong, not merely displayed oddly.

"Reconciliation shows a difference"

The ledger and a subledger disagree. Do not post anything else. Take a backup and report it with a screenshot of that page.

"It says I cannot reverse this"

Read the message — it tells you why. Usually the goods have already been sold, or the document is already reversed. See section 14.

"Stock is right but the value looks wrong"

Remember it is a weighted average, not the last price you paid. Open Stock Valuation and compare the Avg Cost column with your purchases.
